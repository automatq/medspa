What it supports

Goals worth discussing clearly.

  • Dullness
  • Uneven tone
  • Acne-related marks
  • Fine lines and rough texture

Chemical peels use a selected acid or active solution to loosen and shed surface skin cells. Peel depth and formulation must be matched to skin type, tone, condition, and recovery tolerance.

A stronger peel is not automatically a better peel. The right protocol balances the intended change with the risk of irritation and post-inflammatory pigment alteration.

Before you book

Candidacy, preparation, and care after.

01

Who it may suit

Suitability depends on skin tone, barrier health, active conditions, medicines, pregnancy status, recent tanning, and history of pigment change or scarring.

02

How to prepare

Follow only the clinic’s directions for pausing retinoids, acids, exfoliation, hair removal, or other treatments. Avoid tanning and disclose cold-sore history.

03

Downtime & response

Light peels may cause mild redness and flaking. Medium-depth protocols can involve more visible peeling and redness for several days.

04

Aftercare

Do not pick peeling skin. Use gentle barrier care, avoid heat and irritating actives, and apply diligent broad-spectrum sun protection.

Consultation matters: A consultation is required to confirm suitability, treatment scope, expected downtime, and current pricing.

Your questions, answered

Chemical Peels FAQ.

01What concerns can a peel address?

Anima lists uneven tone, sun damage, acne-related marks, fine lines, dullness, and rough texture.

02Will my skin visibly peel?

It depends on peel depth and formulation. Some light peels cause little visible shedding; others produce several days of flaking.

03Are chemical peels suitable for every skin tone?

Many skin tones can be treated, but product choice and preparation must be individualized to reduce pigment-change risk.

04How many peels will I need?

A single treatment may refresh the surface, while a series is often recommended for ongoing concerns.

05Can I combine a peel with other treatments?

Sometimes, but timing matters. Your provider should sequence exfoliation, laser, microneedling, retinoids, and hair removal safely.
