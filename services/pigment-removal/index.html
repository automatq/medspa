Laser treatments · Targeted pigment treatment

Laser Pigment Removal carefully clarified.

A laser treatment for selected benign-looking pigment concerns, planned according to skin tone, pigment type, depth, and the risk of post-treatment colour change.

What it supports

Goals worth discussing clearly.

  • Selected sun spots
  • Freckles
  • Uneven-looking pigment
  • Defined benign pigment lesions

Pigment lasers deliver light that is absorbed by selected pigment. The treated area may darken temporarily before the surface clears as part of healing.

Correct identification matters. Aesthetic laser should not be used on a lesion that is new, changing, suspicious, or not appropriately assessed.

Your appointment

A considered process, step by step.

  1. 01

    Identify

    The provider reviews the pigment pattern, history, skin tone, sun exposure, and medicines.

  2. 02

    Test

    A patch test or medical referral may be recommended before full treatment.

  3. 03

    Target

    Controlled laser pulses are delivered to approved areas.

  4. 04

    Heal

    Temporary darkening, crusting, and gradual clearing are monitored.

Potential benefits

What this treatment may support.

Benefits describe treatment goals, not guaranteed outcomes. Your provider will explain what is realistic for you.

  • Targets selected areas precisely
  • May improve the look of discrete pigment
  • Non-surgical treatment
  • Plan can be adjusted by skin tone and lesion type

Before you book

Candidacy, preparation, and care after.

01

Who it may suit

Only appropriately assessed benign pigment concerns are considered. Tanning, melasma, darker skin tones, medicines, previous pigment change, and lesion uncertainty can affect suitability.

02

How to prepare

Avoid tanning, use sun protection, disclose medications and skin-lightening products, and seek medical assessment for any changing lesion.

03

Downtime & response

Redness, swelling, temporary darkening, crusting, sensitivity, blistering, or lighter/darker pigment change can occur.

04

Aftercare

Do not pick crusts. Keep the area protected, use approved barrier care, and avoid sun exposure with diligent SPF.

Consultation matters: A consultation is required to confirm suitability, treatment scope, expected downtime, and current pricing.

Your questions, answered

Pigment Removal FAQ.

01Which pigment can be treated?

Selected sun-related or benign-looking pigment may be considered after assessment. Not every dark mark is suitable for laser.

02Why can the spot look darker afterward?

Targeted pigment can darken temporarily before the surface sheds or clears during healing.

03Can laser treat melasma?

Melasma can be complex and can worsen with heat or light. It requires careful diagnosis and an individualized plan.

04How many sessions will I need?

The number depends on pigment type, depth, area, skin tone, device response, and healing.

05When should a spot be checked by a doctor?

A new, changing, irregular, symptomatic, bleeding, or otherwise suspicious lesion should be medically assessed before cosmetic treatment.
